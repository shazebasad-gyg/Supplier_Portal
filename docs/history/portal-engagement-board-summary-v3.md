# Supplier Portal Engagement — Board Summary
**Prepared:** April 9, 2026 | **Audience:** Director of Product → Board

---

## Executive Narrative

### The Headline
The supplier portal is deeply embedded in the workflows of GYG's commercially active supplier base. Among suppliers who have transacted on the platform in the last six months — the 46,218 operators who represent the commercially relevant universe — **80.6% logged in within the last 30 days and 91.6% within the last 90 days**. The board-level signal is stronger still: **97.4% of total platform GMV in any given month is generated by suppliers who logged into the portal that month**, a rate that has held between 94.8% and 96.4% for every month of the trailing 12 months.

### The Growth Story
Absolute portal reach is expanding. **52,734 suppliers logged in during March 2026, up 26% year-on-year from 41,786 in April 2025.** The number of suppliers using the portal is growing in absolute terms every month.

### The Concentration Reality
Portal engagement correlates almost perfectly with commercial weight. The top 1% of suppliers by GMV — 505 accounts generating €1.96B (43% of total platform GMV) — log in at 99.2% per month. The top tier combined (€145k+ GMV, ~5,000 suppliers) covers 84% of platform GMV and logs in at 97–99% monthly. The portal works with near-universal reliability for the suppliers that drive GYG's revenue.

### The Activation Opportunity
The area requiring product investment is the entry-level cohort: 15,695 suppliers in the 1–13 bookings/year band who have proven they can generate transactions on GYG but have not yet formed a consistent portal habit. Of these, **5,240 (33%) do not log in monthly**. These are not inactive suppliers — they are transacting operators at an early stage of engagement. Closing this habit gap is the highest-leverage product investment available.

### The North Star Metric
GMV-weighted engagement — the share of total platform GMV generated by logged-in suppliers in a given month — should be the board-level KPI for portal success. It is immune to the composition effects of a growing supplier registry, ties engagement directly to commercial outcomes, and declines only when the suppliers who matter most disengage. Absolute MAU should be tracked alongside it as the reach metric. Together, these two numbers give the board a complete picture: commercial depth and platform breadth.

## Key Takeaways

1. **80.6% of suppliers who have transacted in the last 6 months logged into the portal in the last 30 days.** The engaged supplier base (46,218 with ≥1 booking in L180) logs in at 80.6% L30 and 91.6% L90 — strong engagement rates for a B2B operational platform.

2. **97.4% of platform GMV is generated by suppliers who logged in that month.** This figure has been stable between 94.8% and 96.4% for 12 consecutive months. There is no engagement problem with the suppliers who drive revenue.

3. **Absolute MAU grew +26% year-on-year** — from 41,786 in April 2025 to 52,734 in March 2026. Portal reach is expanding consistently.

4. **Engagement scales tightly with commercial weight.** The top 1% (505 suppliers, €1.96B GMV, 42.8% of platform total) logs in at 99.2% monthly. The top 10% by GMV (~5,000 suppliers, 84% of GMV) logs in at 97%+. Product-market fit with high-value partners is not in question.

5. **Account management and connectivity are the two most powerful engagement levers.** Connected suppliers log in at 57% L30; managed suppliers at 63%; the combination reaches 84%. Yet 85% of the supplier base remains non-managed and non-connected, logging in at 22% L30. The connectivity expansion case is strong — it doubles engagement at no headcount cost.

6. **Engaged suppliers earn materially more net revenue than non-engaged suppliers within the same booking tier.** The within-tier NR premium ranges from 1.3× (entry level) to 3.2× (top tier), indicating that portal engagement is at least partially a driver — not just a symptom — of commercial performance.

7. **The entry-level cohort (1–13 bookings/year) is the primary activation opportunity.** 5,240 suppliers in this band have proven they can generate bookings on GYG but are not regular portal users. Their avg NR is €384/year; engaged peers in the same tier earn €384 vs €287 — a 34% NR premium. This cohort has the scale to move the needle if activated.

8. **Richer engagement signals are planned for end of April 2026.** All current analysis is based on login data. Feature usage, page views, and time-on-site will enable deeper behavioural understanding and help isolate the causal effect of portal engagement on commercial performance.

### Q5: Does account management or connectivity change engagement?

**Data — L30 Login Rate, NR, and Average NR per Supplier by Managed/Connected Status:**

| Type | Suppliers | % of Total | L30 Login Rate | Total NR L365 | Avg NR/Supplier |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Managed + Connected     | 4,030   | 2%    | **84%** | **€616M** | **€161k** |
| Managed + Non-Connected | 5,543   | 3%    | **63%** | €200M  | €56k  |
| Non-Managed + Connected | 19,124  | 10%   | **57%** | €149M  | €9.8k |
| Non-Managed + Non-Connected | 162,565 | **85%** | **22%** | €137M | €3.5k |

**Answer:** Both dimensions are independent engagement drivers. Managed suppliers log in at ~3× the rate of non-managed; connected suppliers at ~2.5× the rate of non-connected. The combination produces the highest engagement at 84%. The key gap: **85% of the supplier base is non-managed and non-connected**, logging in at just 22% L30.

**The connectivity investment case is particularly strong.** Moving a supplier from non-connected to connected (without a dedicated account manager) raises L30 engagement from 22% to 57% — a 2.5× uplift — and is associated with a 2.8× NR increase (€3.5k → €9.8k average). Connectivity integrations are supplier-led, require no headcount scaling, and are the most cost-efficient lever available.

For account management expansion, one AM manages approximately 150–200 suppliers. At a fully-loaded cost of ~€80–100k/year, break-even requires incremental NR well above the engagement gap between managed and unmanaged tiers. The most defensible pilot is expanding AM coverage to Non-Managed + Connected suppliers (19,124 suppliers, 57% L30 already) — the most engagement-ready cohort in the unmanaged pool.

### Q8: Does portal engagement predict commercial performance, or vice versa?

**Data — NR per Supplier: Engaged vs Non-Engaged, within the same booking band:**

| Bookings Band | Engaged (L30) Suppliers | Avg NR (Engaged) | Non-Engaged Suppliers | Avg NR (Non-Engaged) | NR Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 759+ bookings  | 5,925  | **€154,485** | 223   | €48,625 | **3.2×** |
| 122–758 bookings | 8,395 | **€14,555** | 858   | €9,410  | **1.5×** |
| 14–121 bookings | 12,398 | **€2,709**  | 2,945 | €1,793  | **1.5×** |
| 1–13 bookings  | 11,088 | **€384**    | 8,194 | €287    | **1.3×** |
| No bookings    | 15,185 | €0           | 126,051 | €0    | —        |

**Answer:** Engaged suppliers earn materially more NR than non-engaged suppliers **within the same booking tier** — meaning the NR gap is not simply explained by the fact that busier suppliers log in more. The 1.3–3.2× NR premium for engaged suppliers across all tiers is the strongest available signal that portal engagement is at least partially a driver, not just a lagging indicator, of commercial performance.

**Causality note:** Two hypotheses are consistent with the data. First, engagement drives performance: suppliers who use the portal access pricing tools, calendar management, and analytics that improve conversion rates and GMV. Second, performance drives engagement: high-GMV suppliers log in because they have more operational necessity. The within-tier premium — particularly the 3.2× gap in the 759+ tier among suppliers with similar booking volumes but different engagement levels — is more consistent with the first hypothesis. Definitively establishing causality requires a controlled experiment; the planned feature-level engagement data (available end of April 2026) will enable scoping such an experiment.

### Q9: Who are the non-engaging suppliers and what is the right strategic response for each group?

**Data — Breakdown of Suppliers Outside the ≥1 Booking L180 Cohort:**

The 46,218 transacting suppliers are not where the engagement gap lives. The engagement gap lives in the broader registered base. Within the 191,262 total active marketplace suppliers, 145,044 did not generate a booking in the last 180 days. These break into four distinct groups:

| Cohort | Suppliers | % of Non-L180 | Avg Tenure | L30 Login | L365 Login | Avg Lifetime Bookings |
|--------|-----------|---------------|------------|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| Never transacted (lifetime)   | 113,037 | **78%** | 2.1 years | 4.4%  | 42.7% | 0   |
| New (registered ≤90 days)     | 16,830  | 12%    | <0.3 years| 57.1% | 98.1% | 1   |
| Churned (gone dark)           | 8,115   | 6%     | 6.0 years | 0.0%  | 0.0%  | 198 |
| Dormant (active but no recent bookings) | 3,254 | 2% | 3.9 years | 18.0% | 100.0% | 276 |

**Group 1 — Never transacted (113,037 / 78%):** These suppliers have been on the platform for an average of 2.1 years without a single booking. 57% have not logged in at all in the last 12 months. This is structural marketplace composition — suppliers who registered speculatively, couldn't compete in their destination, or operate in low-demand categories. Portal product investment will not change this cohort's outcome. Recommended response: passive maintenance. Automated nurture sequences and listing quality requirements are sufficient; no AM or product resource allocation warranted.

**Group 2 — New suppliers (<90 days, 16,830):** The highest-engagement group in the non-transacting pool at 57% L30. These suppliers are actively onboarding — average of 1 lifetime booking, with the registration-to-first-booking window being the critical habit formation period. Research on B2B SaaS activation consistently shows that users who complete key setup actions in the first 30 days have materially higher long-term retention. Recommended response: invest in the onboarding activation funnel. Identify the specific portal actions (availability setup, first inquiry response, pricing configuration) most correlated with time-to-second-booking, and build guided activation flows around those moments.

**Group 3 — Churned (8,115 / 6%):** Former high-performers (avg 198 lifetime bookings, avg 6-year tenure) who have gone completely silent — 0% login rate at any window. This most likely represents suppliers who have migrated to competing platforms or exited the market. Portal-led interventions will not reach this group. Recommended response: AM-led win-back outreach via email/phone where supplier NR history justifies the investment.

**Group 4 — Dormant (3,254 / 2%):** Relatively high-performers (avg 276 lifetime bookings, avg 4-year tenure) who still visit the portal (18% L30, 100% L365) but have not received a booking in the last year. They are engaged with the portal but facing a demand or supply-side commercial problem — likely ranking deterioration, availability gaps, or seasonal trough. Recommended response: proactive content quality and availability insights delivered through the portal itself; flag to AM for review where applicable.

### Q10: What should GYG's north star metrics for portal engagement be?

**The recommended metric framework:**

**Primary board metric — GMV-weighted engagement rate (L30)**
- Definition: % of total platform GMV L365 generated by suppliers with at least one login in the last 30 days
- Current value: **97.4%**
- 12-month range: 94.8% – 96.4%
- Target floor: ≥95%
- Why: Immune to denominator inflation from supplier registry growth. Declines only when high-GMV suppliers disengage — the actual risk worth monitoring at board level. Does not create perverse incentives around supplier base composition.

**Secondary board metric — Absolute MAU**
- Definition: Unique suppliers with at least one login in the calendar month
- Current value: **52,734** (March 2026), **+26% YoY**
- Why: Shows platform reach expansion without distortion. Grows in absolute terms even as the registry expands.

**Operational metric (product team) — Engaged supplier rate within ≥1 booking L180**
- Definition: L30 login rate among suppliers with at least one booking in the prior 180 days
- Current value: **80.6%**
- Why: Correct denominator for measuring portal product performance against the operators the portal is designed to serve. The L180 window captures seasonal operators; the booking filter removes the structural long tail.

### Limitations & Caveats

- **Login is the only engagement signal currently available.** Page views, feature usage, and time-on-site are not yet captured. A supplier who logs in once counts equally to one who spends hours in the platform. Richer behavioural data is planned for end of April 2026; this will enable a more precise understanding of depth vs. breadth of portal usage.

- **GMV-weighted engagement is computed from `fact_booking.gmv`.** Excludes bookings where `gmv = 0` or `is_fraud = TRUE`. Does not apply a cancellation status filter — cancelled bookings with non-zero GMV may be included. A tighter filter using `status_id` could modestly refine this figure.

- **The 9.3k+ bookings tier shows a lower-than-expected L30 rate (81.8% vs 96.3% in the tier below).** The most likely explanation is large API-connected operators who process bookings programmatically without visiting the portal UI. An API-transaction flag is not currently available in the engagement data; once available, this segment should be excluded from login-based engagement metrics or reported separately.

- **NR per engaged vs non-engaged is correlational.** The within-tier NR premium (1.3–3.2×) is consistent with portal engagement being a causal driver of commercial performance but cannot be confirmed without a controlled experiment. The planned April 2026 feature-level engagement data will enable scoping a causal experiment.

- **AM and connectivity NR comparisons reflect significant selection bias.** Account managers are assigned to high-performing suppliers, not the reverse; the NR gap between managed and unmanaged is not purely a treatment effect. The connectivity ROI case is more defensible because connectivity expansion is supplier-initiated. Both cases should be validated through targeted pilots before scaling investment.

- **The ≥1 booking L180 denominator is a point-in-time snapshot.** Suppliers move in and out of this cohort over time. For quarterly board reporting, a rolling 180-day average denominator is preferable to a single snapshot date. The April 9, 2026 snapshot is representative of current platform state.
