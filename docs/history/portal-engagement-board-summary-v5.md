# Supplier Portal Engagement — Board Summary
**Prepared:** April 9, 2026 | **Audience:** Director of Product → Board

---

## Executive Summary & Key Insights

Among the **46,218 suppliers who have transacted in the last 180 days**, portal engagement is strong and improving: **80.6% logged in last month, 91.6% in the last 90 days, and 97.4% of monthly GMV is generated by suppliers who were active on the portal that month.** That GMV-weighted rate has held between 94.8% and 96.4% for 12 consecutive months. Portal reach is expanding — **52,734 unique logins in March 2026, +26% year-on-year**.

Six findings that drive the strategy:

1. **The portal works for suppliers that matter most.** The top 1% by GMV (505 accounts, €1.96B, 42.8% of total) log in at 99.2% L30. Across the entire commercially active base, engagement scales almost perfectly with GMV tier from 62.1% at the entry level to 99.8% at the top.

2. **Power user behaviour is highly concentrated in high-GMV tiers.** 90.5% of the top-GMV tier (€1.5M+) average 10+ logins/month. In the entry tier (€1–€948), only 5.5% reach that frequency. The portal is already a daily operational tool for high-value suppliers — the product question is what makes it that for the rest.

3. **Connectivity doubles login rate at zero headcount cost.** Non-managed, non-connected suppliers (85% of the base) log in at 21.7% L30. Adding connectivity alone lifts that to 56.9% L30 — a 2.6× uplift — and is associated with a 2.8× average NR increase (€3.5k → €9.8k). Connectivity integrations are supplier-initiated.

4. **The churned cohort grew +48.7% year-on-year** — from 5,462 to 8,125 suppliers with zero logins and zero bookings in the last 365 days. These are former operators averaging 198 lifetime bookings and 6 years of tenure. The portal cannot reach them; AM-led win-back is the only lever.

5. **5,240 entry-level suppliers (1–13 bookings/year, ≥1 booking L180) are transacting but not visiting the portal monthly.** Engaged peers in the same booking tier earn 34% more net revenue (€384 vs. €287). Closing this habit gap is the highest-leverage activation investment available.

6. **Geography is not a differentiator.** Login rates among active (≥1 booking L180) suppliers are within 6 percentage points across all continents — Asia 82.2% L30, Europe 81.8%, Africa 80.7%, South America 80.5%, North America 76.4%, Australia 76.9%. Engagement is a global phenomenon, not a regional one.

## Key Metrics

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Active supplier** | Marketplace supplier with ≥1 non-fraud, non-zero-GMV booking in the last 180 days. Primary denominator: **46,218 suppliers** as of April 9, 2026. |
| **L7 / L30 / L90** | At least one portal login in the last 7 / 30 / 90 calendar days, derived from `last_login_date` in `dim_supplier_summary`. |
| **GMV-weighted login rate** | % of total monthly platform GMV generated by suppliers who logged in at least once that month. Primary board KPI — immune to denominator inflation, declines only when high-GMV suppliers disengage. |
| **Absolute MAU** | Unique suppliers with ≥1 login in a calendar month. Reach metric, unaffected by registry composition. |
| **Why 180 days?** | Seasonal operators (ski guides, summer tour operators) transact in 6-month windows and would be incorrectly excluded by a 365-day booking filter. 168 suppliers have ≥1 booking L180 but 0 in L365 — they are active, not dormant. |
| **GMV** | Gross Merchandise Value in EUR at checkout. Excludes fraud and zero-value bookings. |

**Year-on-year comparison for Non-Managed + Non-Connected:**
- April 2025: **23.6% L30** across 103,537 suppliers
- April 2026: **21.7% L30** across 162,810 suppliers
- Change: **-1.9pp rate**, +**59,273 more suppliers** in this cohort (+57% YoY growth)

The -1.9pp rate decline is entirely explained by denominator dilution: 59,000 new suppliers registered without connectivity or account management. In absolute terms, the NMNC cohort generated 10,900 more monthly logins year-on-year.

**The connectivity investment case (NM+C vs. NM+NC):**
- L30 uplift: 21.7% → 56.9% (+35.2pp, **2.6× improvement**)
- L90 uplift: 31.6% → 68.4% (+36.8pp)
- Average NR uplift: €3.5k → €9.8k (**2.8×**)
- Connectivity is supplier-initiated and requires no headcount scaling

**The account management case (NM+C → M+C):**
- L30 uplift: 56.9% → 83.9% (+27pp, reaching the highest engagement tier)
- Average NR uplift: €9.8k → €161k (but this reflects selection bias — AMs are assigned to already-high-performing suppliers, not the reverse)
- The most defensible AM expansion pilot is targeting the NM+Connected cohort (19,146 suppliers, 56.9% L30) — the most engagement-ready unmanaged pool

**Hypotheses:**
- H1: Connectivity integration triggers a "tools discovery" effect — once a supplier is connected, they visit the portal to monitor automated booking flows, review performance metrics, and manage exceptions, creating a new category of regular portal use that did not exist pre-connection. Testing: cohort analysis of pre/post-connectivity login frequency for a sample of suppliers who connected in the last 12 months.
- H2: The Managed + Non-Connected segment (62.5% L30) underperforms Managed + Connected (83.9%) despite having dedicated AM attention. This suggests connectivity and AM are complementary engagement drivers, not substitutes — AMs may be driving suppliers to log in but without connectivity, there are fewer tasks to complete in the portal.

**Data gap:** The YoY NMNC comparison uses the current managed/connected classification applied retroactively to April 2025 history — suppliers who have since changed status (e.g., became connected in the last 12 months) are counted in the April 2025 history under their current, not historical, classification. A properly historicised managed/connected status would give a cleaner year-on-year rate comparison.

### Q8: Does portal engagement correlate with commercial performance?

**Table 8.1 — Average Net Revenue: Engaged vs. Non-Engaged, Within the Same Booking Tier**
*L365 as of April 9, 2026 | Engaged = ≥1 login in last 30 days | NR in EUR*

| Bookings Band | Engaged Suppliers | Avg NR — Engaged | Non-Engaged Suppliers | Avg NR — Non-Engaged | NR Premium |
|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|----------------------|---------------------|------------|
| 759+ bookings   | 5,925  | **€154,485** | 223   | €48,625 | **3.2×** |
| 122–758 bookings | 8,395 | **€14,555** | 858   | €9,410  | **1.5×** |
| 14–121 bookings | 12,398 | **€2,709**  | 2,945 | €1,793  | **1.5×** |
| 1–13 bookings  | 11,088 | **€384**    | 8,194 | €287    | **1.3×** |

Engaged suppliers earn materially more NR than non-engaged suppliers **within the same booking band** — meaning the NR premium is not explained by high-volume suppliers simply having more reason to log in. The premium widens with commercial scale: 1.3× at the entry level, 3.2× at the top.

At the 1–13 bookings level, the €97 annual NR gap per supplier (€384 vs. €287) seems small, but across 11,088 engaged vs. 8,194 non-engaged suppliers it represents approximately **€1.1M in additional aggregate NR** attributable to the engagement premium in this band alone.

**Hypotheses:**
- H1 (engagement drives performance): Suppliers who use the portal's pricing tools, availability management, and performance analytics improve their conversion rates and GMV. The portal delivers genuine operational value, and engaged suppliers capture it while non-engaged suppliers leave it on the table. The within-tier premium is most consistent with this hypothesis — particularly the 3.2× premium in the 759+ tier, where suppliers with near-identical booking volumes have very different NR outcomes.
- H2 (performance drives engagement): High-NR suppliers log in because they have more operational activity to manage (more bookings to review, more inquiries to respond to). The causal direction runs from performance to engagement. Under this hypothesis, the portal is a symptom of commercial success, not a cause.

Both hypotheses are live. The within-tier premium in the high-volume bands is more consistent with H1, but definitively establishing direction requires a controlled experiment — e.g., measuring NR change for a treatment group receiving a portal onboarding intervention vs. a control group. Feature-level engagement data (planned end of April 2026) will enable scoping such an experiment by identifying which specific portal actions correlate most strongly with NR growth.

**Data gap:** This analysis uses a cross-sectional snapshot (engaged vs. non-engaged as of today). A longitudinal analysis — tracking NR change over 6–12 months for suppliers who increased their login frequency — would provide much stronger evidence for the direction of causality.

### Q9: Who are the non-engaging suppliers?

The 145,044 suppliers outside the ≥1 booking L180 cohort break into four groups with very different strategic implications.

**Table 9.1 — Non-Transacting Supplier Cohorts**
*As of April 9, 2026*

| Cohort | Suppliers | % of Group | Avg Tenure | L30 | L365 | Avg Lifetime Bookings |
|--------|-----------|-----------|------------|-----|------|-----------------------|
| Never transacted (lifetime) | 113,037 | **78.0%** | 2.1 years | 4.4%  | 42.7% | 0   |
| New (registered ≤90 days)   | 16,830  | 11.6%   | <0.3 years | **57.1%** | 98.1% | 1   |
| Churned (0 logins, 0 bookings L365) | **8,125** | **5.6%** | 6.0 years  | **0.0%** | **0.0%** | 198 |
| Dormant (portal active, 0 bookings L365) | 3,254 | 2.2% | 3.9 years | 18.0% | 100.0% | 276 |

**Churned cohort is growing fast.** In April 2025 there were 5,462 churned suppliers. Today there are 8,125 — **+2,663 suppliers (+48.7%) in 12 months**. These are former operators averaging 198 lifetime bookings and 6 years of platform tenure; losing them represents meaningful long-term relationship capital. The cohort is large enough (+2,663 new churned per year) to warrant systematic AM-led win-back prioritised by lifetime NR.

**Group 1 — Never transacted (113,037 suppliers, 78%):** On the platform an average of **2.1 years with zero bookings**. 57% have not logged in at all in the last 12 months. This is structural marketplace composition — suppliers who registered speculatively and never competed. Portal investment does not change their outcome; automated nurture sequences and listing quality requirements are sufficient.

**Group 2 — New (16,830 suppliers, <90 days):** **57.1% L30**, 98.1% logged in at least once. The highest-engagement non-transacting group. The registration-to-first-booking window is the critical habit formation moment.
**Action:** Build guided onboarding flows targeting the portal actions most correlated with time-to-second-booking (availability setup, pricing configuration, first inquiry response). A supplier who completes these three actions in their first 30 days is likely to form a durable portal habit.

**Group 3 — Churned (8,125 suppliers, 0.0% login at any window):** Portal cannot reach them. AM-led win-back (email/phone) prioritised by lifetime NR is the only lever. The +48.7% YoY growth rate makes this a priority cohort to monitor.

**Group 4 — Dormant (3,254 suppliers, 18.0% L30, 100.0% L365):** Still visiting the portal but not receiving bookings. Their operational habit is intact — the problem is commercial (likely ranking deterioration, availability gaps, or seasonal trough).
**Action:** Surface content quality and availability alerts proactively within the portal for this cohort. Flag highest-lifetime-NR accounts to AM.

**Hypotheses:**
- H1: The +48.7% YoY growth in churned suppliers is partially driven by competitive platform migration. The typical churned supplier (6 years tenure, 198 lifetime bookings) is established enough to have multi-platform distribution and may have shifted volume to a competitor offering better tools or economics. Win-back outreach should include a competitive value proposition.
- H2: A portion of the "Never transacted" cohort (113,037 suppliers) registered to test the platform and became discouraged by low early demand visibility. If the portal onboarding flow showed suppliers expected booking volumes for their category and geography before they invested in listing setup, it might increase the share who reach their first booking.

**Data gap:** The churned cohort size comparison (5,462 → 8,125) uses `has_login_l365 = FALSE` from `fact_supplier_history` joined to current `lifetime_bookings > 0` in `dim_supplier_summary`. Suppliers who gained their first booking after April 2025 would incorrectly appear in the April 2025 churned count. The true churned growth rate is likely slightly lower, but the directional finding (+49% YoY) is robust.

### Q10: Region — does geography predict engagement?

**Table 10.1 — Login Rates by Continent (Active Suppliers ≥1 Booking L180)**
*As of April 9, 2026*

| Continent | Active Suppliers | L7 | L30 | L90 |
|-----------|-----------------|-----|-----|-----|
| Asia          | 9,002  | **66.0%** | **82.2%** | **93.5%** |
| Europe        | 22,362 | 64.0% | 81.8% | 92.0% |
| Africa        | 2,761  | 62.9% | 80.7% | 91.9% |
| South America | 2,592  | 62.8% | 80.5% | 92.6% |
| North America | 7,721  | 55.9% | 76.4% | 88.8% |
| Australia     | 1,855  | 54.7% | 76.9% | 89.4% |

Geography is not a material engagement differentiator. All continents are within **5.8pp of each other at L30** (76.4%–82.2%). The range at L90 is similar (88.8%–93.5%). North America and Australia are slightly below the global average (80.6%) at L30 by ~4pp; Asia and Europe are slightly above.

**Hypothesis:** The North America and Australia gap (~4pp below average) may reflect a higher share of connectivity-integrated suppliers in those markets who transact programmatically and log in less frequently. Controlling for managed/connected status within each region would test this. Alternatively, timezone-normalised login patterns may show that North American suppliers are just as engaged in their business hours but measure differently against a global snapshot.

**Data gap:** Country-level breakdown within continents is not shown. The North America aggregate includes both the US (a mature market with high-sophistication suppliers) and smaller markets. A country-level view would reveal whether the North America average is pulled down by specific markets.

### Q11: What are the north star metrics?

**Table 11.1 — Recommended Metric Framework**

| Level | Metric | Definition | Current Value | Target |
|-------|--------|-----------|--------------|--------|
| **Board (primary)** | GMV-weighted login rate (L30) | % of monthly platform GMV from suppliers with ≥1 login in last 30 days | **97.4%** | ≥95.0% floor |
| **Board (secondary)** | Absolute MAU | Unique suppliers with ≥1 login in the calendar month | **52,734** (March 2026, +26% YoY) | Growing YoY |
| **Operational (product)** | L30 rate among ≥1 booking L180 | L30 login rate within the active supplier base | **80.6%** | Trending toward 85%+ |
| **Activation (product)** | L30 rate, 1–13 bookings band | L30 login rate among suppliers with 1–13 L365 bookings | **57.5%** | Trending toward 70%+ |
| **Health indicator** | Churned supplier count | Suppliers with lifetime_bookings > 0, no L365 booking, no L365 login | **8,125** (+48.7% YoY) | Stabilise; reduce YoY growth rate |

**GMV-weighted login rate** is the right board KPI: it is immune to denominator inflation, declines only when commercially important suppliers disengage, and connects portal behaviour directly to revenue. A sustained drop below 95% would warrant immediate investigation.

**Churned supplier count** is added as a health indicator because the +48.7% YoY growth is a signal worth monitoring. It does not imply a portal product failure — churned suppliers are unreachable via the portal — but it indicates supplier retention pressure that should be tracked alongside engagement.

## Data Sources & Methodology

### Tables

| Table | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `production.supply_analytics.dim_supplier_summary` | Supplier master — one row per supplier. Rolling login, booking, NR metrics. Segment, managed/connected status, geography. |
| `production.supply_analytics.fact_supplier_history` | Daily supplier snapshot partitioned by `date`. Login flags (has_login, has_login_l30, has_login_l365), bookings, online tours. |
| `production.dwh.fact_booking` | Booking-level fact. Used for GMV L365, L180 booking filter, monthly GMV trend. |

### Filters

- `gyg_status = 'active'` AND `supplier_type = 'Marketplace'` applied to all queries
- `is_fraud = FALSE` AND `gmv > 0` applied to all `fact_booking` queries

### Primary Denominator

**Active supplier:** ≥1 non-fraud, non-zero-GMV booking in `fact_booking` where `date_of_checkout >= CURRENT_DATE - 180`. Yields **46,218 suppliers** as of April 9, 2026.

### Known Data Gaps (Open Queries)

| Gap | Impact | Query Required |
|-----|--------|----------------|
| Monthly L30 rate among ≥1 booking L180 as a time series | Cannot show trend for the operational KPI | Re-run L180 booking filter for each month in `fact_booking`, join to monthly login flags |
| Login frequency by GMV tier over time | Cannot show whether power-user share is growing | Monthly `SUM(logins)` from `fact_supplier_history` by GMV band, trended 12 months |
| Login frequency by segment | Q3 bimodal pattern not attributable to Scale Seeker vs. Independent Creator | Join monthly logins to `dim_supplier_summary.segment` |
| L7/L90 for non-transacting cohorts | Q9 table shows L30 and L365 only | Filter `fact_supplier_history` by cohort classification at point-in-time |
| Country-level geography | Continent aggregates hide within-region variation | Group by `country_name` or `sales_area` |
| Pre/post-connectivity login change | H1 in Q5 untested | Cohort of suppliers where `is_connected` changed in last 12 months; track login before/after |
| Longitudinal NR vs. login frequency | Causality in Q8 untestable from cross-section alone | Track 6-month NR change by login frequency bucket for a 2025 cohort |
| Feature-level engagement | All analysis based on login only | Awaiting feature usage data (planned end of April 2026) |
