# Supplier Portal Engagement — Board Summary
**Prepared:** April 9, 2026 | **Audience:** Director of Product → Board

---

## Executive Narrative

### The Good News
The portal has near-universal adoption among the suppliers that drive GYG's revenue. The top 10% of suppliers by GMV — 5,056 accounts generating 84% of all platform GMV — log in at 90%+ L30. The top 1% (506 suppliers, €1.96B GMV) log in at 99%. **Product-market fit with high-value partners is not in question.**

### The Concern
The 28% headline MAU rate is a breadth metric being pulled down by a large inactive long tail. 74% of "active" suppliers have zero GMV and zero bookings in the last 365 days. The MAU rate has declined from 31% (Apr 2025) to 28% (Mar 2026) because the supplier base is growing faster than the engaged user base. In absolute terms, MAUs grew +26% YoY — but this is hidden by the denominator problem.

### The Strategic Choice
This is a question of **depth vs breadth**. GYG currently measures itself on a breadth metric (headline MAU rate) but operates as a depth platform (the top 10% generate 84% of GMV). The data suggests two legitimate paths:

- **Depth strategy:** Accept the long tail as natural marketplace churn, redefine the success metric to GMV-weighted engagement, and concentrate resources on the 5,000–20,000 suppliers that drive commercial outcomes.
- **Breadth strategy:** Invest aggressively in activating the 140k+ inactive suppliers, accepting a long payback period on the theory that more engaged suppliers mean greater supply diversity and better customer selection over time.

### The Recommendation
**Adopt a tiered strategy weighted toward depth, with a targeted activation programme for three specific sub-cohorts of the long tail.**

The data does not support equal investment across all inactive suppliers. The 74% zero-GMV group is not homogeneous — it contains a recoverable minority (new suppliers still onboarding, dormant suppliers with strong histories) buried under an irreducible majority (suppliers who have never transacted and show no signs of starting). See Section 5 for the full breakdown.

**Proposed north star metric:** Replace headline MAU rate with **GMV-weighted engagement rate** — the share of total platform GMV generated by suppliers who logged in at least once in the last 30 days. Currently ~99% of GMV comes from suppliers who have logged in at least once in the last 90 days. This metric rewards engagement where it matters commercially and is not distorted by dormant supplier accumulation.

## Key Takeaways

> **The portal works extremely well for the suppliers that drive GYG's revenue — but overall engagement is suppressed by a large inactive long tail that requires disaggregation before any strategic response is possible.**

1. **Top 1% of suppliers (506 accounts, €1.5M+ GMV/year) log in at near-100% rates.** These 506 suppliers generate €1.96B GMV — 43% of total platform GMV. Universal portal adoption at the top is a strong signal of product-market fit with high-value partners.

2. **Top 10% by GMV generate 84% of all GMV and have 90%+ L30 login rates.** The portal is deeply embedded in the workflows of the suppliers that matter most commercially.

3. **The 28% headline MAU rate understates true engagement among active suppliers.** 74% of "active" suppliers have zero GMV in the last year — largely dormant, never-transacted, or new. Excluding them, L30 engagement among transacting suppliers ranges from 53% (entry-level) to 99% (top 1%).

4. **MAU rate is declining as the platform scales.** It fell from ~31% (Apr 2025) to ~25% (Dec 2025) before recovering to ~28% (Mar 2026). Absolute MAUs grew +26% YoY (41.8k → 52.7k), but the supplier base is growing faster.

5. **The 74% zero-GMV cohort is not homogeneous — only ~14% of it represents a recoverable activation opportunity.** New suppliers (<90 days, 16.8k) and dormant-but-active suppliers (3.3k) are recoverable. The remaining 86% are either churned silent partners (8.1k, impossible to reactivate via portal) or suppliers who have never transacted in their 2+ year platform lifetime (113k, structural long tail).

6. **Engagement correlates almost perfectly with GMV tier AND with NR per supplier.** Engaged suppliers in every booking band earn 1.3–3.2× more NR than non-engaged suppliers in the same band. This is the strongest available indicator that portal engagement is a driver — not just a symptom — of commercial performance.

7. **Account management and connectivity are the highest-leverage interventions — but reach only 15% of the base.** Connectivity alone drives L30 engagement from 22% to 57%. Adding management on top pushes it to 84%. Expanding connectivity is cheaper and faster than expanding the AM headcount and should be prioritised first.

8. **Richer engagement signals are planned for end of April 2026.** All current analysis is based solely on login data. Feature usage, page views, and time-on-site will enable deeper behavioural understanding.

**Answer:** Mixed picture. **Absolute MAUs are up +26% YoY** (41.8k → 52.7k) — a healthy absolute growth in engaged users. However the **MAU rate has declined from ~31% to ~28%** because the supplier base is growing faster than the engaged user base. **Financial framing of the trend:** The MAU rate decline of 3.4pp since April 2025 represents approximately 6,400 suppliers who would be engaged today if the rate had held. Engaged suppliers in the entry-level booking band (1–13 bookings/year) generate ~€384 NR each; at the mid-tier (14–121 bookings), ~€2,700 each. The commercial cost of the engagement rate decline is therefore in the range of €2–17M annual NR depending on the quality of the disengaged population — a yellow flag rather than a crisis, but one that warrants monitoring. The more significant risk is structural: if the rate continues declining toward 20%, the gap between headline engagement and actual commercial engagement will widen further, making it harder to diagnose problems and justify product investments in the portal.

The concern is not the absolute level — it is the declining trend and the heavy concentration of engagement at the top.

### Q5: Does account management or connectivity change engagement?

**Data — L30 Login Rate, NR, and Average NR per supplier by Managed / Connected Status:**

| Type | Suppliers | % of Total | L30 Login Rate | Total NR L365 | Avg NR/Supplier |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Managed + Connected     | 4,030   | 2%    | **84%** | **€616M** | **€161k** |
| Managed + Non-Connected | 5,543   | 3%    | **63%** | €200M | €56k |
| Non-Managed + Connected | 19,124  | 10%   | **57%** | €149M | €9.8k |
| Non-Managed + Non-Connected | 162,565 | **85%** | **22%** | €137M | €3.5k |

**Answer:** Both dimensions are independent engagement drivers. Managed suppliers log in at ~3× the rate of non-managed; connected suppliers at ~2.5× the rate of non-connected. The combination drives the highest engagement (84%). The key gap: **85% of the supplier base is non-managed and non-connected, with only 22% L30 engagement and €3.5k avg NR per supplier.**

**ROI case for expanding coverage:** The NR gap between managed and non-managed is large (€161k vs €3.5k avg), but this reflects selection bias — account managers are assigned to high-performing suppliers, not the reverse. The more defensible ROI case is for **connectivity expansion**: moving a supplier from Non-Connected to Connected (without a dedicated AM) raises L30 engagement from 22% to 57% and is associated with a 2.8× NR uplift (€3.5k → €9.8k avg). Connectivity integrations are supplier-led and require no headcount scaling.

For account management expansion, a conservative model: one AM manages approximately 150–200 suppliers. At a fully-loaded cost of ~€80–100k/year per AM, break-even requires ~€500–700 in incremental NR per managed supplier. Non-Managed+Connected suppliers earn €9.8k avg NR today; if adding management improves engagement from 57% to 84% and converts ~20% of that engagement gap into incremental NR (a conservative causal assumption), the incremental NR per managed supplier is well above break-even. A focused AM expansion pilot targeting 500–1,000 Non-Managed+Connected suppliers (the most engagement-ready cohort) would generate a definitive ROI signal within two quarters.

### Q8: Does portal engagement predict commercial performance, or vice versa?

**Data — NR per supplier: Engaged vs Non-Engaged, within the same booking band:**

| Bookings Band | Engaged (L30) Suppliers | Avg NR (Engaged) | Non-Engaged Suppliers | Avg NR (Non-Engaged) | NR Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 759+ bookings | 5,925 | **€154,485** | 223 | €48,625 | **3.2×** |
| 122–758 bookings | 8,395 | **€14,555** | 858 | €9,410 | **1.5×** |
| 14–121 bookings | 12,398 | **€2,709** | 2,945 | €1,793 | **1.5×** |
| 1–13 bookings | 11,088 | **€384** | 8,194 | €287 | **1.3×** |
| No bookings | 15,185 | €0 | 126,051 | €0 | — |

**Answer:** Engaged suppliers earn materially more NR than non-engaged suppliers **within the same booking tier** — i.e., the NR gap is not simply explained by the fact that busier suppliers log in more. The 1.3–3.2× NR premium for engaged suppliers across tiers is the strongest available indicator that portal engagement is partially a driver, not just a lagging indicator, of commercial performance.

**Causality discussion:** Two hypotheses are consistent with the data:
1. **Engagement drives performance:** Suppliers who use the portal actively access pricing tools, calendar management, customer communications, and analytics that improve their conversion rate and GMV. This would mean expanding engagement directly grows NR.
2. **Performance drives engagement:** High-GMV suppliers log in because they have more operational necessity — more bookings to manage, more notifications to respond to. In this case, engagement is a symptom and the real lever is driving initial bookings via search ranking, marketing support, or onboarding quality.

The within-tier NR premium (particularly the 3.2× gap in the 759+ tier) is more consistent with hypothesis 1 — suppliers with similar booking volumes but different engagement levels have systematically different NR, suggesting the portal itself contributes value beyond just booking management. However, definitively distinguishing causality requires a controlled experiment (e.g., a portal feature experiment measuring NR uplift). The planned April 2026 feature-level engagement data will help scope such an experiment.

### Q9: Who exactly are the 74% zero-GMV suppliers — and what is the right strategic response for each group?

**Data — Breakdown of the 141,236 zero-GMV suppliers:**

| Cohort | Suppliers | % of Zero-GMV | Avg Tenure | L30 Login | L365 Login | Avg Lifetime Bookings |
|--------|-----------|---------------|------------|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| Never transacted (lifetime) | 113,037 | **80%** | 2.1 years | 4.4% | 42.7% | 0 |
| New (registered ≤90 days) | 16,830 | 12% | <0.3 years | **57.1%** | 98.1% | 1 |
| Churned (gone dark) | 8,115 | 6% | 6.0 years | 0.0% | 0.0% | 198 |
| Dormant (active but no bookings) | 3,254 | 2% | 3.9 years | 18.0% | 100.0% | 276 |

**Answer:** The 74% zero-GMV cohort is not a single problem — it is four different problems requiring four different responses:

**Group 1 — Never transacted (113,037 / 80%):** These suppliers have been on the platform for an average of 2.1 years without ever generating a booking. 57% have never logged in during the last 12 months. This cohort is almost certainly structural marketplace churn — suppliers who registered speculatively, couldn't compete, or operate in low-demand destinations. Investing heavily in activating this group is unlikely to generate meaningful commercial returns. Recommended action: passive. Maintain listing quality requirements and automated nurture sequences; do not allocate AM or product resources.

**Group 2 — New suppliers (<90 days, 16,830):** This is the highest-engagement cohort in the zero-GMV pool at 57% L30. They have an average of 1 lifetime booking, meaning many are actively onboarding. This is an **onboarding activation opportunity** — the window between registration and first meaningful booking is critical for habit formation. Research on B2B SaaS activation (Hulick, Chameleon.io) consistently shows that users who complete key setup actions in the first 30 days have materially higher long-term retention and expansion rates. Recommended action: audit the onboarding funnel for this cohort; identify the portal actions (availability setup, pricing, first response to an inquiry) most correlated with time-to-first-booking; and build guided activation flows around those moments.

**Group 3 — Churned (8,115 / 6%):** These are former high-performers (avg 198 lifetime bookings, avg 6-year tenure) who have gone completely silent — 0% login rate at any window. This likely represents suppliers who moved to competing platforms, exited the market, or experienced a service quality issue significant enough to cause complete disengagement. Their historical performance (198 bookings) makes them commercially interesting to reactivate but portal-led interventions will not work at 0% engagement. Recommended action: route to AM-led win-back outreach via email/phone, not portal product.

**Group 4 — Dormant (3,254 / 2%):** These are relatively high-performers (avg 276 lifetime bookings, avg 4-year tenure) who still log in (18% L30, 100% L365) but have not received a booking in the last year. They are still engaged with the portal but are experiencing a commercial supply problem — likely reduced demand, ranking deterioration, or seasonal factors. Recommended action: proactive content quality and availability insights; flag to AM for review where applicable.

### Q10: What should GYG's north star metric for portal engagement be?

**Current metric:** Headline MAU rate (all active suppliers) — 27.7% in March 2026, declining trend.

**Problem with the current metric:**
- Distorted by the 113,037 "never transacted" suppliers pulling the denominator up
- Does not distinguish between engagement among high-value partners and engagement among dormant long-tail
- Creates perverse incentives: removing inactive suppliers improves the metric without improving the business

**Proposed replacement — GMV-weighted engagement rate:**
- Definition: % of total platform GMV generated by suppliers who logged in at least once in the last 30 days
- Current estimated value: ~97–98% (the vast majority of GMV-generating suppliers are highly engaged)
- This metric is immune to denominator inflation, directly ties engagement to commercial outcomes, and declines only when high-GMV suppliers disengage — which is the actual risk worth monitoring

**Alternative — Transacting supplier engagement rate:**
- Definition: L30 login rate among suppliers with at least 1 booking in the last 365 days
- Current value: ~65–70% (estimated from existing data)
- Simpler to communicate and still filters out the inactive long tail

**Recommendation:** Adopt GMV-weighted engagement rate as the primary board-level metric, with transacting supplier engagement rate as the operational complement. Retain headline MAU as a secondary signal for tracking absolute reach. Deprecate headline MAU rate as a headline KPI at the board level.

### Tables Used

| Table | Catalog / Schema | Description |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|
| `production.supply_analytics.dim_supplier_summary` | production.supply_analytics | Supplier master data — one row per supplier, current snapshot. Includes lifetime and rolling metrics (bookings, NR, tours, reviews, logins). |

### Filters Applied

| Filter | Value | Reason |
|--------|-------|--------|
| `dss.gyg_status = 'active'` | Active only | Excludes deleted / deactivated suppliers |
| `dss.supplier_type = 'Marketplace'` | Marketplace only | Excludes T&T (GYG own-inventory) — not portal-relevant |
| `fsh.is_active = TRUE` | Where applicable | Ensures supplier was active on that date for MAU rate calculations |
| `fb.is_fraud = FALSE` | fact_booking only | Excludes fraudulent transactions from GMV |
| `fb.gmv > 0` | fact_booking only | Excludes cancelled / zero-value bookings from GMV aggregation |

### Metric Definitions

| Metric | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| **Active supplier** | `gyg_status = 'active'` AND `supplier_type = 'Marketplace'` |
| **MAU** | Unique suppliers with at least one login in the calendar month |
| **MAU Rate** | MAU ÷ total active suppliers on the platform that month |
| **GMV-weighted engagement rate** | % of total platform GMV L365 generated by suppliers with `last_login_date >= CURRENT_DATE - 30` |
| **GMV** | Gross Merchandise Value in EUR (`fact_booking.gmv`) — total amount paid by customers, converted to EUR at time of booking. Excludes fraud and zero-value bookings. |
| **L7 / L30 / L90** | At least one login in the last 7 / 30 / 90 calendar days respectively |
| **Login** | A supplier portal login session as recorded in `fact_supplier_history.logins` |
| **Managed** | Supplier has a dedicated GYG account manager (`dim_supplier_summary.is_managed = 'Managed'`) |
| **Connected** | Supplier uses a connectivity system / API integration (`dim_supplier_summary.is_connected = 'Connected'`) |
| **Dormant** | Zero `bookings_l365`, non-zero `lifetime_bookings`, has logged in within the last 365 days |
| **Churned** | Zero `bookings_l365`, non-zero `lifetime_bookings`, no login in the last 365 days |
| **Never transacted** | Zero `lifetime_bookings` |
| **New** | Registered within the last 90 days |

### Limitations & Caveats

- **Login is the only engagement signal currently available.** Page views, feature usage, and time-on-site are not yet captured. Richer behavioural data is planned for end of April 2026.
- **74% of "active" suppliers have zero GMV / bookings in the last 365 days.** Of these, 80% have never transacted in their platform lifetime. The 28% headline MAU rate is significantly influenced by this inactive denominator.
- **GMV is computed from `fact_booking.gmv` (EUR-converted at checkout).** Excludes bookings where `gmv = 0` or `is_fraud = TRUE`. Does not apply status filtering beyond this — cancelled bookings with non-zero GMV may be included; a tighter filter using `status_id` could refine this further.
- **NR per engaged vs non-engaged is correlational.** The within-tier NR premium for engaged suppliers (1.3–3.2×) is consistent with portal engagement being a causal driver of commercial performance, but cannot be confirmed without a controlled experiment. Causality research is recommended for Q2/Q3 2026 once feature-level engagement data is available.
- **AM ROI estimates are directional.** The NR gap between managed and non-managed suppliers reflects significant selection bias (AMs are assigned to high-performing suppliers). The AM expansion ROI model uses a conservative 20% causal attribution of the NR gap; actual attribution should be validated via a pilot programme before scaling investment.
- **Cohort segmentation of the zero-GMV population uses current snapshot data.** Seasonality cannot be directly identified from the current query structure — a supplier who transacts only in summer may appear "never transacted" if their last booking was >365 days ago. The April 2026 feature engagement data release will enable more precise seasonal cohort analysis.
